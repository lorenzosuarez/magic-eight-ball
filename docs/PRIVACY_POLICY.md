# Privacy Policy for Magic AI Ball (Wear OS and Android phones and tablets)

**Effective date:** RELEASE-DATE

This policy explains how the Magic AI Ball app (package `com.lorenzosuarez.magiceightball`), developed by Lorenzo Suarez, handles information. It covers both versions of the app: the one for Wear OS watches and the one for Android phones and tablets. Where they differ, this policy says so. The app is for adults: people aged 18 or over.

## Summary
- No account is needed. On phones and tablets you can sign in with Google if you want to, as described under "Your account"; the watch app has no sign-in.
- On phones and tablets, and only with your consent where the law requires it, the app counts how it is used (never what you ask or what it answers), as described under "Usage measurement". The watch app measures nothing. If the app crashes, a technical crash report is sent to the developer, as described under "Crash reports". The watch app shows no ads. The phone and tablet app may show an occasional ad, as described under "Ads"; you are asked for consent first where the law requires it.
- Without an account, the only things the app saves are on your device: your choices in the app (language, character, whether to use AI answers and, on phones and tablets, whether to use voice questions and spoken answers, and the names you type for party mode) and, on phones and tablets, a list of your latest questions and answers ("Recents"). The only server the developer runs is the one that checks a Premium purchase, described under "Premium": without a purchase, it receives nothing from you.
- **With an account**, Google's Firebase service keeps for the developer what Google gives the app when you sign in (your name, your email address, the address of your profile photo and an account identifier), the profile you fill in (your first name and date of birth) and the characters you create. An account is kept only once you have filled in the profile and agreed, on the screen that says so, that your first name, your zodiac sign, the fact that it is your birthday (on the day only) and the name and description of a character you created are sent to the AI service, so that answers can be made for you. Your date of birth itself, your email address and your photo are never part of what the AI is asked. You can delete the account, and everything kept with it, at any time.
- **If you buy Premium**, which the phone and tablet app may offer as a single payment through Google Play, Google Play takes the payment and the app never sees your card or any other payment details. A server the developer runs checks the purchase with Google Play and keeps, with your account, the date, Google Play's order number and a fingerprint of the purchase, as described under "Premium".
- **Adults only:** the app is for people aged 18 or over, as described under "Age".
- An answer leaves the app for another app or person only when you choose "Share" under it, as described under "Sharing an answer".
- You can switch AI answers off in the app at any time.
- The app connects to Google services, all described below: Firebase Remote Config, to check its settings; the Gemini API, reached through Firebase AI Logic, when AI answers are switched on; Firebase App Check, to show that a request comes from this app; Firebase Crashlytics, when the app crashes; and, on phones and tablets, Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore if you sign in, Firebase Cloud Messaging for notifications, Google Analytics for Firebase when your consent allows it, Google AdMob while ads are switched on and, for Premium, Google Play's billing system and Cloud Functions for Firebase.
- **Asking out loud:** on both versions you can hold and say your question. The app uses the microphone only while it is listening to a question you started, your speech is turned into text by your device's speech recognition service, and that text is sent to the Gemini API only when AI answers are switched on. The app never stores your voice. On phones and tablets the text of your question is saved, with its answer, only in the "Recents" list on your device, which you can clear at any time; the watch saves nothing. The app works without the microphone.
- **On the watch** holding to ask is offered only while AI answers are in use, because only the AI service can answer a question; otherwise the watch answers to a shake and never listens.

## What the app sends, and to whom
**Firebase Remote Config.** When the app starts, and at most about once an hour while you use it, it asks Google's Firebase Remote Config service for its current settings, such as whether AI answers are switched on. This request includes a random installation identifier created by Firebase for this copy of the app, and basic app and device information (for example app version, language, country and operating system version). It does not include your name, account or location. Google handles this under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

**Gemini API.** AI answers are used only when both you and the developer have them switched on: you choose with "AI answers" in the app's options (on by default), and the developer can switch them on or off for everyone. When the developer has them switched off, that page shows them as not available. While AI answers are off, by your choice or the developer's, the app shows answers from a list built into the app and sends nothing to the Gemini API. The same is true while the "Classic" character is chosen: its answers are always the classic ones built into the app, whatever else is switched on. And it is true on a device where the date of birth entered was that of someone under 18, as described under "Age". While AI answers are on, asking the ball sends a request over an encrypted HTTPS connection to Google's Gemini API, through Google's Firebase AI Logic service. What the app puts in that request is only:
- an instruction written by the developer, which reflects the character and answer language (English, Spanish or Portuguese) you chose in the app, together with what the app draws at random for each answer so that no two are alike: whether it is to be a yes, a no or undecided, and a theme and a turn of phrase taken from lists built into the app;
- when you shake, or tap on a phone or tablet: the fixed word "shake";
- when you asked out loud and your question was recognised: the text of that question (at most 200 characters) instead of the fixed word, so that the answer responds to it;
- on phones and tablets, and only if you are signed in and agreed to it as described under "Your account": your first name, cut down to its letters (at most two words and 20 characters, or six characters for a name written in Chinese, Japanese or Korean), only when "Therapist", "Grandma", "Coach", "Lovestruck" or a character you created answers; your zodiac sign, only when "Horoscope" or "Mystical" answers; on your birthday, the fact that it is today, whichever character answers, and only until one answer written by AI has been given that day; and, when a character you created answers, its name, down to its letters and digits, and your description of it, on one line and without brackets, curved quotation marks or characters that cannot be seen, and never its colour or its accessory. They are added to the instruction;
- technical generation settings, and what tells Google which app and which of the developer's Firebase projects the request comes from.

Without an account, or while you are signed out, nothing in the instruction comes from you. On both versions the request never includes audio, the names typed for party mode, your contacts, location, device identifiers or sensor readings, and the app never puts your date of birth, your email address or your photo in it. When you are signed in, Firebase's software in the app attaches to the request the token that shows which account is signed in, as it does to every request it makes for someone who is signed in. Firebase uses that token to know the account; it can carry the account's name, email address and photo address as Google issued them, and it is not part of the instruction or of the question. Please do not say anything in a question, or write anything in a character's description, that you would not want to send to Google. As with any internet request, Google receives your device's IP address and standard connection information. Google handles these requests under the [Gemini API Additional Terms of Service](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms), the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy) and the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). The developer uses the paid service: under its terms, Google does not use these requests or the answers to improve its products, and logs them for a limited time, only to detect misuse and where the law requires it.

If the request fails or your device is offline, the app shows an answer from the built-in list instead. The same happens once a device has asked for its daily allowance of AI answers; to keep that count, the app saves today's date and a number on your device, and nothing else.

Requests to the Gemini API, to the account services described below and to the server that checks a Premium purchase also carry a short-lived token from Google's Firebase App Check service. To issue it, Google Play's integrity service checks that the app is the one published on Google Play and that it is running on a genuine Android device. The token is used only to tell whether a request comes from this app.

## Your account (phones and tablets only)
The app works without an account. On phones and tablets you can sign in, and what this section describes happens only if you do. The watch app has no sign-in and shows nothing that belongs to an account.

- **Signing in.** You sign in with a Google Account, and with nothing else, through Google's Firebase Authentication service. The first time you sign in is what creates your account in the app. Google gives the app your name, your email address, the address of your profile photo and an identifier for the account. The app never sees your Google password and receives nothing else from your Google Account. To protect accounts, Firebase Authentication also records the IP address and the kind of device and app used to sign in, and keeps that record for a few weeks.
- **Your profile.** Once you are signed in, the app asks for your first name and your date of birth. There is no account without them: if you choose "Continue without an account", the app signs you out and deletes the account that signing in had just created, and nothing is kept; if the account cannot be deleted at that moment, for example without a connection, you stay signed in and the app tells you so, so that you can try again. An account can remain without a profile when the app is closed on that screen and never opened again: it then holds only what Google gave the app when you signed in, and the developer deletes such accounts by hand. Your date of birth is used for three things only: to check that you are 18 or over, to work out your zodiac sign and to know when it is your birthday. It is kept with your account. The date itself is never sent to the AI service; on your birthday, the request says that the day is today, as described next.
- **Answers made for you.** The screen that asks for your profile also says what will be sent to the AI service, and the button that completes it is your agreement to that. "Continue without an account" is how you decline, and then no account is kept. With that agreement, the request described under "Gemini API" carries your first name, your zodiac sign or the fact that today is your birthday and, when a character you created answers, its name and your description of it, each only as listed there. There are two ways to stop that, both under "Account" in the options: signing out stops the sending at once and keeps your account; deleting the account stops it and deletes your profile and the record of your agreement. Correcting your profile later does not change that record, which keeps the date on which you agreed; and if what the screen says is sent ever changes, the app asks you again.
- **Characters you create.** With an account you can create characters of your own, at most five: a name, a description in your own words of how the character answers, and the colour and the accessory you pick for it. While Premium is offered, creating a new one is for those who have bought it, as described under "Premium"; the ones you already have stay yours either way. They are private: nobody else who uses the app can see them. The screen where you make one says, before you save it, that it is kept in your account and sent to the AI service when it answers. When one of them answers, and you have agreed as described above, its name and its description are sent to the AI service so that the answer is in its voice; its colour and its accessory are never sent. This happens only while you are signed in, the app has read your profile and accounts are switched on, which the developer can switch off for everyone. Otherwise nothing of the character is sent and it answers from the classic answers built into the app, as it does whenever AI answers are off; when you sign out, it stops being the character chosen. Please write nothing in a description, about yourself or anyone else, that you would not want to send to Google.
- **Where it is kept.** What Google gave the app when you signed in is kept by Firebase Authentication, which Google runs in the United States. Your profile, the record of what you agreed to and your characters are kept by Cloud Firestore, in Google's data centres in the European Union. Google runs both on the developer's behalf, under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy). Through the app, only you, signed in, can read or change your profile and your characters. The developer can reach them with Firebase's administration tools, and does so only to keep the service running or to answer a request from you.
- **What it is never used for.** Nothing kept with your account is used for ads or given to the ad service. The app adds none of it to the crash reports or to the usage counts, which are not tied to your account: a character you created appears in them only as your own, never by its name or description. An answer you choose to report is sent as it was written, so one that was made for you may contain your first name.
- **Signing out.** "Sign out", under "Account" in the options, signs this device out. Your account, your profile and your characters stay as they are for the next time you sign in.
- **Your control.** Under "Account" you can see and correct your profile. You can edit any of your characters from the list of characters, and delete one from the screen where you edit it: after you confirm, it is gone from your account at once. To ask for a copy of what your account holds, use the contact at the end of this policy.
- **Deleting your account.** "Delete account", under "Account" in the options, deletes your profile and all your characters together, and then the account itself. If you bought Premium, the record of that purchase is deleted too, as described under "Premium". If you no longer have the app, the page [Delete your account](https://magic-ai-8-ball.web.app/delete-account) explains how to ask for the same. What remains afterwards, and for how long, is described under "Retention and deletion".

## Premium (phones and tablets only)
The phone and tablet app may offer Premium, the one thing it sells: a single payment made through Google Play, not a subscription. Premium lets you create characters of your own, gives you a larger daily allowance of AI answers and takes the ads away, as described under "Ads". It can be bought only while you are signed in and have a profile. The watch app sells nothing.

- **Asking Google Play.** While Premium is offered and you are signed in, the app asks Google Play's billing system on your device for the price and for any purchase of Premium made with the Google Play account on that device. Otherwise it does not contact Google Play's billing system at all.
- **Paying.** Google Play takes the payment, on its own screen. The app never sees or keeps your card or any other payment details. With the purchase, the app gives Google Play a fingerprint (SHA-256) of your app account's identifier, which lets Google Play tell one buyer from another and does not say who you are. Google handles the purchase under the [Google Play Terms of Service](https://play.google.com/about/play-terms/) and the [Google Payments Privacy Notice](https://payments.google.com/payments/apis-secure/get_legal_document?ldt=privacynotice).
- **Checking the purchase.** The app does not take its own word for a purchase. Google Play gives it a purchase token, a code that stands for that purchase, and the app sends the token to a server the developer runs on Google's Cloud Functions for Firebase, in the European Union. The server asks Google Play whether the purchase is paid for. The app sends the token when you buy, and again whenever you are signed in and the check is still owed: when your app account does not hold the purchase yet, for example on a new phone or in a new account, or when Google Play has not yet been told that the purchase was delivered. Like every request Firebase's software makes for someone who is signed in, it carries the token that shows which account is signed in; it carries nothing of your profile or your characters. As with any internet request, Google receives your device's IP address and standard connection information.
- **What is kept.** In Cloud Firestore, in the European Union, the server keeps with your account the date and time Premium was granted, Google Play's order number and a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the purchase token; and, apart from your account, which account holds that purchase and since when. The purchase token itself is not stored and not written to any log: the server's log says only how each check ended. Through the app, only you, signed in, can read whether your account has Premium, and nobody can change it: only the server does. Under "Account", the app shows "You have Premium" when it does. Google runs the server and the database on the developer's behalf, under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).
- **One account at a time.** A purchase is held by one account of the app at a time, the first that claims it. If you are signed in to another account, the app tells you that Premium is held by another account, and you are not charged again.
- **Refunds and cancellations.** Once a day the server asks Google Play which purchases it has refunded or cancelled, and deletes what it kept of those. Premium then ends for that account; the characters already made stay in it.
- **What it is never used for.** Nothing about a purchase is sent to the AI service, used for ads or given to the ad service, and the app adds nothing about it to the crash reports or to the usage counts.
- **When the account is deleted.** The server deletes what it kept with the account and the record of which account held the purchase. The purchase itself stays in your Google Play account: that record is Google's, under Google's policy, and deleting the app account does not refund it. If you later create a new account in the app on a device signed in to the same Google Play account, the app claims the purchase for the new account, without charging you again, as long as Premium is still offered.

## Crash reports
If the app stops working unexpectedly, it sends a crash report to Google's Firebase Crashlytics service the next time it is opened, so that the developer can find and fix the fault. Both versions of the app do this.

- **What a report contains.** The technical trace of the failure (where in the app's code it happened), the app version, the device model, the operating system version, the device's language and orientation, free memory and storage at the time, the date and time, and a random installation identifier created by Crashlytics for this copy of the app.
- **What it never contains.** Your questions, the ball's answers, your "Recents" list, audio, your name, account or location. The app adds nothing of its own to a crash report. (An answer you choose to report is sent separately: see "Reporting an answer".)
- **Who receives it.** Google, on the developer's behalf, under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy). Reports are kept for 90 days and then deleted. They are used only to fix the app, never for ads.
- **When the app has not crashed.** Each time the app is opened it sends Crashlytics a short session record (app version, device model, operating system version and the same random installation identifier), which is what lets the developer see how many sessions end without a crash. It contains nothing about what you do in the app.

## Usage measurement (phones and tablets only)
To learn which parts of the app are used and whether a change made it better, the phone and tablet app counts a few things you do in it, with Google Analytics for Firebase. The watch app does not.

- **What is counted.** That the ball answered, and for that answer: whether you tapped, shook, spoke or used "Which of you…?", the character in use, whether the answer was written by AI or came from the built-in list, and whether it had a second line. That you marked an answer as good or bad: which of the two, never the answer. That you shared an answer or the app. That you chose a character or turned one of the switches in the options, and which. That party mode was set up, and for how many players. That you watched an ad through for more AI answers, opened the app from the day's verdict, or answered Android's question about notifications, and which way.
- **What is never counted.** Your questions, the ball's answers, the names typed for party mode, your "Recents" list, audio, anything kept with your account, or anything else you said or wrote. The app has no way to put them in what it counts.
- **What goes with it.** Google Analytics adds what it collects for every app: a random identifier created for this copy of the app, the app version, the device model and operating system, the language and country, and when and for how long the app was used. The app has switched off its collection of your advertising ID and of the device's own ID.
- **Your consent.** Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), nothing is counted unless you agreed to it in the consent message, to storing information on your device, to measuring and to improving the service. Until that message has been answered, nothing is counted either. You can change your choice at any time under "Ad privacy choices" in the options, and the counting follows it.
- **Not for advertising.** The app tells Google Analytics not to use these counts for advertising or for personalising ads.
- **Who receives it.** Google, on the developer's behalf, under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy). The counts are kept for at most 14 months.
- **Shared links.** The Google Play link the app puts in what you share says that it was shared from the app, so the developer can see how many installs come from sharing. It says nothing about you.

## Notifications (phones and tablets only)
The developer can send the app's users a notification now and then, about something new in the app or as a reminder to come back, through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging. The watch app receives none.

- **The day's verdict.** Once a day the app can show a line in the voice of the character you chose. It is made on your device from the answers built into the app: nothing is sent or fetched for it, and it is not shown on a day you have already asked the ball. You can switch it off under "Daily verdict" in the options.
- **Your permission.** On Android 13 and later the app shows no notification unless you allow it. It asks once, and only after you have used the app on several days. You can change your answer at any time in your device's settings, under the app's notifications.
- **How a message reaches you.** Messages are sent to topics, not to people: every copy of the app follows one topic common to all and one for the language it is set to, so a message can be written in your language. To deliver them, Firebase Cloud Messaging gives this copy of the app a registration token, tied to a random installation identifier created by Firebase. Neither says who you are, and the app sends nothing of your questions, answers or choices with them, apart from the language.
- **Who receives it.** Google, on the developer's behalf, under the [Firebase privacy and security terms](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

## Ads (phones and tablets only)
The phone and tablet app can show a full-screen ad from time to time, only after you have closed an answer and never while you are asking or reading one. The developer can switch ads on and off for everyone; while they are off, nothing described in this section happens. The watch app never shows ads.

- **An ad you choose to watch.** Once you have used the day's AI answers, the options may offer more of them in exchange for watching an ad. It is shown only if you ask for it, and it is requested and handled like the other ads described here.
- **With Premium.** If your account has Premium, the app shows you no ad and does not offer the one for more answers. Google's advertising software is started, and ads are requested, only when you close an answer and not when the app opens, so that the app has first read from your account whether it has Premium; once it has, neither happens. The consent message described below is still shown where the law requires it, because it also decides whether the app's use is measured, as described under "Usage measurement"; the way back to that choice in the options is then called "Privacy choices".
- **Who shows them.** Ads are provided by Google AdMob. To request and show an ad, Google's advertising software in the app collects and uses information such as your device's advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from it, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google handles it under [its policy on how it uses information from apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites) and the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).
- **Your consent.** Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), the app asks for your choice before any ad is requested, through Google's consent message. You can change that choice at any time under "Ad privacy choices" in the app's settings. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or turn off ad personalisation, in your device's settings.
- **What ads never get.** Your questions, the ball's answers and your "Recents" list are never used for ads or shared with the ad service by the app.
- **Permission.** The advertising software adds the `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID` permission to the phone and tablet app.

## Voice questions
On a phone or tablet you hold the ball and say your question; on the watch you hold the screen. This is optional. On a phone or tablet you can switch "Voice questions" off in the options; on either device you can decline the microphone permission, and then the app answers to a tap or a shake only. The watch offers it only while AI answers are in use.

- **When the microphone is used.** Only while you are holding to ask (or, on a phone or tablet with an accessibility service, between choosing "Ask by voice" and the end of your question), and only while the app is on screen. The app does not listen at any other time and never in the background.
- **Who turns your speech into text.** The app does not process or record audio itself. It hands the listening to the speech recognition service of your device, through Android's standard speech recognition feature. Where your device offers on-device recognition for the app's language (Android 12 and later), the app uses it, and the audio is processed on the device. Otherwise the app uses your device's default speech recognition provider, which is typically Google and which may send the audio to its own servers to recognise it. That provider handles the audio under its own privacy policy (for Google, the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)); you can see or change the provider in your device's settings.
- **What happens to the text.** The recognised question is shown on screen, written as a question: a recogniser hears words and not the tone they were said in, so the app adds the question marks itself. If AI answers are switched on, it is sent to the Gemini API as described above; if they are off or not available, it is not sent anywhere by the app (and the watch does not listen at all).
- **What is kept.** Audio is never saved. On phones and tablets the recognised question is saved, together with its answer, in the "Recents" list described below, on your device only. The watch keeps the question in memory only until the answer has been shown.

## Spoken answers (phones and tablets only)
The phone and tablet app can read each answer out loud. This is off until you switch "Spoken answers" on in the options, and you can also have a single answer read with the speaker button shown while it is on screen.

- **Who speaks.** The app does not produce the voice itself. It hands the text of the answer, and nothing else, to the text-to-speech service of your device, through Android's standard feature. That service usually speaks on the device; depending on the voice installed, it may send the text to its provider's servers to produce the audio. The provider (typically Google) handles it under its own privacy policy, and you can see or change it in your device's settings.
- **What is never handed over.** Your question, your "Recents" list and the names typed for party mode. An answer that is one of those names, as described under "Party mode", is read out like any other answer.
- **What is kept.** Nothing: the app saves no audio.

## Party mode (phones and tablets only)
In the options you can type the names of the people playing, from two to eight. The app then shows whose turn it is and offers "Which of you…?", which answers with one of those names picked at random on the device.

- **Where the names are.** In the app's settings file on your device, and in "Recents" when a name was an answer. They are not sent to the developer, to the Gemini API or to anyone else, and "Which of you…?" contacts no service at all.
- **Your control.** "Turn off" in the party mode dialog deletes the names. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app does too.
- Please type first names or nicknames only, and only of people who are playing with you.

## Sharing an answer (phones and tablets only)
Under an answer, "Share" makes a picture of it and hands it to the app you pick from your device's share menu. Nothing is shared unless you choose it, each time.

- **What is in the picture.** The app's name, your question if you asked one out loud, the answer with the line the character added under it, and the name of the character that gave it (for a character you created, the name you gave it).
- **What goes with it.** A short text with the app's Google Play link.
- **Where it goes.** Only to the app you pick, which handles it under its own terms. The developer receives nothing, and the app does not learn which app you picked.
- **What is kept.** The picture is saved as one file in the app's temporary storage so that the other app can read it, and is replaced by the next one you share. It is removed when you clear the app's cache or data or uninstall the app, and your device may remove it on its own.

## Reporting an answer

Answers written by AI are not read by anyone before you see them. Under such an answer there is a "Report" button, for when one should not have been given.

- **What is sent.** The answer as it was written, with the line the character added under it on phones and tablets, which character gave it (one of your own only as such, never by name), the language and, where the app asks what is wrong with the answer, the reason you pick from a short list. Your question is not sent, and the app adds nothing kept with your account and nothing that identifies you; an answer made for you may itself contain your first name.
- **Who receives it.** The developer, through Firebase Crashlytics, the same Google service that receives crash reports and under the same terms and retention described under "Crash reports". It is used only to review what the AI wrote and to correct the instructions it is given.
- **Your choice.** Nothing is sent unless you press "Report". A report cannot be taken back once sent.

## Home screen widget and watch tile
The phone and tablet app offers a home screen widget, and the watch app a tile, which show an answer when touched. Their answers always come from the list built into the app: they send nothing to the Gemini API, use no microphone, and are not saved in "Recents".

## Recents: your latest questions and answers (phones and tablets only)
The phone and tablet app keeps a list of the latest answers the ball gave you, so you can read them again. The watch app keeps no such list.

- **What is saved.** For each answer: the text of your question if you asked out loud (nothing for a tap or a shake; for "Which of you…?" in party mode, those words and the name that was picked), the answer, the character that gave it (for a character you created, its name, which stays in the list even if you later delete the character or your account), the date and time, and whether the answer was written by AI or came from the app's built-in list.
- **Where.** In a private file in the app's own storage on your device. It is not sent to the developer, to Google or to anyone else, it is not synchronised between devices, and it is not included in device backups or device-to-device transfers.
- **How much.** At most the latest 50 answers; older ones are deleted automatically as new ones arrive.
- **Your control.** You can delete any single entry (with its delete button; "Undo" brings it back for a few seconds), or all of them with "Delete all", from the list itself. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app also deletes the list.
- **Sharing.** The list itself is never sent anywhere. An answer on screen can be shared by you as described under "Sharing an answer". "Share the app" in the options hands only a short text with the app's Google Play link to the app you pick.

## Sensors and permissions
- **Motion sensor (accelerometer):** used only on your device to detect a shake and, on phones and tablets, to move the ball slightly as you move the device. Sensor readings are not stored or sent anywhere. No permission is required.
- **Internet and network state** (`INTERNET`, `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`): used to contact Firebase Remote Config, the Gemini API, Firebase App Check, Firebase Crashlytics after a crash and, on phones and tablets, Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore while you are signed in, Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google Analytics for Firebase when your consent allows it, Google AdMob while ads are switched on and the server that checks a Premium purchase.
- **Other apps on your device:** the app asks Android which speech recognition and text-to-speech services are installed, only to be able to listen to a question and to read answers out loud. It reads nothing else about your apps.
- **Notifications** (`POST_NOTIFICATIONS`): asked for once, as described under "Notifications". The app works the same without it.
- **Microphone** (`RECORD_AUDIO`): requested the first time you hold to ask out loud, and used only as described under "Voice questions". You can refuse it or take it back in your device's settings at any time; the app keeps working by tap and shake.
- **Google Play billing** (`com.android.vending.BILLING`): on phones and tablets, added by Google Play's billing system, which the app uses only as described under "Premium". Nothing is asked of you.

Neither version requests location, camera, contacts, body sensors or any other sensitive permission.

## Information stored on your device
The app saves your choices (language, character, whether to use AI answers and, on phones and tablets, whether to use voice questions and spoken answers, and the names typed for party mode) in a small settings file on your device, so they are still there the next time you open the app. They stay on the device, are not sent anywhere on their own, and are removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall it. On phones and tablets the app also saves the "Recents" list described above (at most your latest 50 questions and answers), which is removed the same way or from the list itself. On phones and tablets the app also keeps how many days in a row you have asked the ball, as two numbers and a date, which never leave the device. On phones and tablets the picture of the last answer you shared waits in temporary storage, as described under "Sharing an answer". On phones and tablets, while you are signed in, Firebase's sign-in software in the app keeps on the device what keeps you signed in: what Google gave the app when you signed in (your name, your email address, the address of your profile photo and the account identifier) and the tokens that show the account is yours. It is removed when you sign out or delete the account. Your profile and your characters are not copied to the device: the app reads them from Cloud Firestore each time it starts and holds them in memory only, and every change to them is made on the server or not at all. There is one exception: the character of your own that is chosen to answer is kept whole in the app's settings file (its name, its description, and which colour and accessory it has), so that it answers without a connection. That copy is replaced when you change the character, and removed when you choose another character, when you delete that one, and when you sign out or delete your account. Nor does the app keep anything of a Premium purchase on the device: whether your account has Premium is read from Cloud Firestore while you are signed in, and the purchase itself is held by Google Play. The app does keep on the device the year in which it last wished you a happy birthday (the year only, not the day): it stays after you sign out or delete your account, and is removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall the app. Where a date of birth of someone under 18 was entered, the app keeps a mark of that on the device, and nothing of what was typed. On the watch, the latest answer is kept in memory only. The app stores nothing else of its own. Firebase stores its installation identifiers and the latest settings on the device, and a crash report waits there until it has been sent. Nothing is included in device backups.

## Retention and deletion
Without an account, the developer collects and stores nothing about you apart from the crash reports described above and any answer you chose to report, which Google keeps for the developer for 90 days, and the usage counts described under "Usage measurement", which it keeps for at most 14 months. What the app saves on your device stays there until you delete it, as described above.

With an account, your account, your profile and your characters are kept for as long as the account exists, and the record of a Premium purchase for as long as the account exists and Google Play has not refunded or cancelled the purchase. When you delete the account in the app, they are deleted at once, and the server deletes the record of a purchase as soon as the account is gone; when you ask for it as the page [Delete your account](https://magic-ai-8-ball.web.app/delete-account) explains, within 30 days. The developer keeps no copy. Google then removes them from its own systems and backups, which for what it gave the app when you signed in takes up to 180 days, and keeps its record of the IP addresses used to sign in for a few weeks. Deleting the account does not delete what is on your device ("Recents" and your choices), the crash reports and usage counts, which are not tied to your account, what the AI service logged of requests already made, or a Premium purchase itself, which stays in your Google Play account.

Information received by Google, by your device's speech recognition or text-to-speech provider, or by an app you share an answer with, is handled under their policies as described above.

## Age
The app is for people aged 18 or over. It is not directed at children or teenagers, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. The profile asks for a date of birth: when the date entered is that of someone under 18, the account created at sign-in is deleted and nothing they typed is saved. From then on the app on that device answers only from the answers built into it, and sends no request to the AI service. If the account cannot be reached to be deleted at that moment, the device is marked all the same: the app asks the AI service for nothing, sends nothing of the profile, and tries the deletion again the next time it is opened. If you believe someone under 18 has an account, tell the developer through the contact below and it will be deleted.
